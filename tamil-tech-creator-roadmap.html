Where the Tamil tech space has a hole

Four clusters exist today. You belong in the fourth, which is nearly empty.

Gadget channels

Tamil Tech (~4.5M), Tech Boss, A2D. Phone reviews. Trained the audience to expect energetic Tanglish and loud thumbnails. Not competition.

Tutorial channels that are edtech funnels

Error Makes Clever (1M+), GUVI, Tamil Programmer. Beginner playlists that end in a bootcamp pitch. They do not talk about working in tech, packages, or specific companies.

Job-update aggregators

Sharmilan Vipokan and similar. “Zoho hiring, apply link”, guest interview experiences, Telegram and WhatsApp funnels, paid mentoring. High volume, low depth, dependent on search.

Working engineers on camera

CS in Tamil (Google engineer), Data Engineering Tamil. Credible, but infrequent and no career/interview/vlog content. This is the cluster you take over.

Nobody in Tamil combines: proof of a strong outcome, candid named-company insight, and interview-depth teaching (system design, DSA patterns). The Zoho videos ranking today are 3–6 years old and fresher-only. Zoho cancelled 2026 hikes in October — the topic is hot right now.

Positioning

Others are teachers or aggregators. You are the insider friend who already made it, showing the real game in Tamil.

Proof on screen

Redacted offer letter, LeetCode profile, interview calendar. Tamil viewers are sceptical of “26 LPA” claims — receipts beat charisma.

Specific and named

Real round structures, real salary bands, what actually gets asked. Aggregators stay generic to avoid risk; you can be specific because you lived it.

Depth, not basics

EMC owns “what is a for loop”. You own “I can code — how do I get 15–30 LPA”. That segment in Chennai is bigger than it looks.

Channel promise (bio + trailer):

“Zoho-la start panni 26 LPA varaikum — nadanthadhu, nadakkaadhadhu, nadakka vendiyadhu. Tech career in Tamil, no sugar coating.”

90-day timeline

Front-load the Zoho series so you launch with a hook and a buffer, then settle into a rhythm you can sustain alongside a full-time job.

  1. Week 0 — prep, nothing public
    • Record the 60-second trailer: who you are, the 26 LPA proof, what you will post.
    • Batch-shoot all five Zoho videos. Edit two before publishing anything.
    • Set up Telegram or WhatsApp community link, Instagram handle, channel art, playlists (empty is fine).
    • Re-read your exit agreement. Decide the no-go list: internal code, unreleased products, named colleagues.
  2. Weeks 1–2 — the Zoho launch series
    • Publish five videos, Mon/Thu, in the order listed under “First 30 videos”.
    • Cut 8–10 Shorts/Reels from them: one claim each, hook in 1.5 s, Tamil on-screen text, “full video channel-la”.
    • Pin the trailer and the Zoho playlist on the channel page.

    Goal: first 500 subscribers and the Zoho playlist ranking in search.

  3. Weeks 3–7 — weekly rhythm
    • Mon interview/skill video, Thu career/AI/news video, Sat vlog, 4–5 Shorts/Reels, one Instagram carousel.
    • Start the two flagship playlists: “DSA in Tamil – 30 Patterns” and “System Design in Tamil”.
    • Reply to every comment for the first 60 days. Comments are the heaviest engagement signal.
  4. Week 8 — first review
    • Check 30-second hold, average retention, CTR, Shorts completion, Telegram joins.
    • Double down on the two best-performing formats; drop or rework the worst one.
    • Ask the community (poll) which company series they want next.
  5. Weeks 9–12 — compound
    • Launch the monthly live mock interview with a viewer (free). Nobody in Tamil does this.
    • Second company series (Freshworks, Chargebee, or a service MNC — whichever the poll picks).
    • Collaborate once with a mid-size Tamil career or coding creator (podcast-style, 30 min).

    Goal by day 90: 60+ public videos, 3 playlists with 5+ videos, measurable Shorts → long-form conversion, 1,000+ community members.

Shorts & Reels playbook

Shorts and long-form rank on separate engines now: Shorts bring strangers in; long-form keeps them and earns. Treat Shorts as top of funnel, not as a channel of their own.

YouTube Shorts

  • Target completion: 65% for under-30 s, 50% for 30–60 s. Under 30 s is the default.
  • Original only: your face, your claim, your edit. Re-uploads lose reach since October 2026.
  • Set Related Video on every Short to its parent long-form.
  • Aim for 0.5–1% Shorts → long-form conversion; most channels sit at 0.04%.
  • Pin a Community post that routes new visitors to the Zoho playlist.

Instagram

  • Saves and shares are the strongest signals. Educational Reels and carousels earn the most saves.
  • Post Reels natively (no watermark); Instagram deprioritises obvious cross-posts.
  • 15–30 s Reels for growth; 60–90 s allowed when teaching.
  • One carousel a week: round structure, 6-month roadmap, pattern cheat-sheet.
  • Stories: behind the scenes, polls on next topic. Low reach, high loyalty.
Short formulas that fit you: “Interviewer asked me this at [company]” · “Salary myth #N” · “One AI thing that saved me an hour today” · “Zoho fact nobody tells freshers” · “DSA pattern in 25 seconds”. Hook in 1.5 seconds with Tamil on-screen text; end with “full video channel-la”.

Monetisation path

Months 1–6

Nothing. No affiliates, no course. Build trust and watch hours. Your edge is being the insider, not the institute.

Months 6–12

Paid mock interviews and resume reviews (Topmate-style). Monthly live mock stays free. Channel memberships once eligible.

Year 2

A small cohort on “Zoho → product company switch”. Keep it small and outcome-based so it never turns the channel into a funnel.

Rules that protect the channel

Legal and reputation

  • Zoho content covers process, culture, growth, compensation ranges, and your decision. Never internal code, unreleased products, or named individuals.
  • Receipts for every number you claim. Redact, but show.
  • Disagree with companies, not people.

Sustainability

  • Vlog footage: 3–4 usable minutes a day is enough. Do not shoot all day.
  • News gets views; interview content gets subscribers. Keep the ratio.
  • Two-video buffer at all times. Never publish on the day you edit.
  • Reply to comments for 20 minutes a day, then stop.
