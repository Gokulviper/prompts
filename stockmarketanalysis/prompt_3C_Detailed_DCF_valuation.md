# Prompt 3C: Detailed DCF Valuation

Act as a senior equity valuation analyst.

Perform a complete Discounted Cash Flow valuation for:

Company: [COMPANY NAME]
Ticker: [TICKER]

Investment horizon: [HORIZON]

## 1. Historical cash flow analysis

Collect 5 years of:
- Revenue.
- EBIT.
- Tax rate.
- Depreciation and amortization.
- Capital expenditure.
- Change in working capital.
- Operating cash flow.
- Free cash flow.

Calculate historical unlevered free cash flow.

## 2. Forecast assumptions

Build a 5–10 year forecast.

Forecast:
- Revenue by segment.
- Revenue growth.
- EBIT margin.
- Effective tax rate.
- Depreciation.
- Capital expenditure.
- Working capital requirements.
- Unlevered free cash flow.

Explain every assumption.

## 3. WACC calculation

Calculate:
- Risk-free rate.
- Equity risk premium.
- Levered beta.
- Cost of equity.
- Pre-tax cost of debt.
- After-tax cost of debt.
- Capital structure.
- Weighted average cost of capital.

Explain the sources and methodology.

## 4. Terminal value

Calculate using:

A. Gordon Growth Model.

B. Exit Multiple Method.

Explain the differences and limitations.

## 5. Valuation scenarios

Create:
- Bear case.
- Base case.
- Bull case.

Use different revenue growth, margins, WACC and terminal growth assumptions.

## 6. Sensitivity analysis

Build:
- WACC vs terminal growth.
- Revenue growth vs operating margin.
- CapEx vs FCF.
- Terminal multiple vs fair value.

## 7. Equity valuation

Calculate:

Enterprise Value
Less: Net Debt
Less: Other senior claims where applicable
Equals: Equity Value

Divide by fully diluted shares outstanding.

Calculate fair value per share.

Account for:
- Options.
- RSUs.
- Convertible securities.
- New share issuance.
- Expected dilution.

## 8. Margin of safety

Calculate:
- Intrinsic value.
- Current share price.
- Upside/downside.
- 10%, 20%, 25% and 30% margin-of-safety purchase prices.

## 9. Final conclusion

Explain:
- Whether the company is undervalued or overvalued under each scenario.
- Which assumptions have the greatest impact.
- Which metrics investors should monitor.

Use current market data and official filings.

Do not force a positive valuation conclusion. Clearly state uncertainty and avoid false precision.
