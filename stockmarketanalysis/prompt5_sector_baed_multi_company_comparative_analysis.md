# Prompt 5: Sector-Based Multi-Company Comparative Analysis

Act as a senior institutional equity research analyst and sector investment strategist.

I have completed four independent research modules for each of the following companies:

1. Company A: [NAME]
2. Company B: [NAME]
3. Company C: [NAME]
4. Company D: [NAME]

I will provide the following research documents for each company:

- Complete Excel Valuation Model.
- Five-Year Financial Statement Analysis.
- Detailed DCF Valuation.
- Competitor Valuation Comparison.
- Individual Investment Committee Report.

Your task is to perform an independent comparative investment analysis across all companies.

IMPORTANT:
Do not simply summarize their individual reports.
Challenge their assumptions, normalize financial metrics and perform independent comparisons.

## 1. Sector overview

- Industry structure.
- Total addressable market.
- Industry growth rate.
- Industry trends.
- Technology disruption.
- Regulatory risks.
- Long-term growth opportunities.

## 2. Business model comparison

Compare:
- Revenue streams.
- Recurring revenue.
- Customer concentration.
- Revenue diversification.
- Competitive moat.
- Switching costs.
- Pricing power.
- Market positioning.

## 3. Five-year financial comparison

Create a standardized comparison table.

Compare:
- Revenue CAGR.
- Gross margin.
- Operating margin.
- Net margin.
- EPS CAGR.
- ROE.
- ROIC.
- Operating cash flow.
- Free cash flow.
- Capital expenditure.
- Debt.
- Cash position.

Ensure all companies use comparable financial periods and definitions.

## 4. Financial quality comparison

Evaluate:
- Earnings quality.
- Cash conversion.
- Debt sustainability.
- Capital efficiency.
- Working capital management.
- Shareholder dilution.
- Capital allocation.

## 5. Growth comparison

Compare:
- Historical growth.
- Future revenue projections.
- EPS growth.
- Market expansion.
- New products.
- AI opportunities.
- Management guidance.

Identify which companies have the strongest growth drivers and the greatest execution risks.

## 6. Valuation comparison

Standardize:
- Trailing P/E.
- Forward P/E.
- EV/EBITDA.
- EV/Revenue.
- Price-to-free-cash-flow.
- DCF fair value.
- Historical valuation multiples.

Explain why companies deserve different valuation multiples.

Do not blindly compare different business models using the same multiple.

## 7. DCF comparison

Compare:
- WACC.
- Revenue growth assumptions.
- Terminal growth.
- Operating margins.
- CapEx.
- Terminal value contribution.
- Intrinsic value.

Challenge unrealistic assumptions.

## 8. Risk comparison

Compare:
- Business risks.
- Financial risks.
- Debt exposure.
- Customer concentration.
- Regulatory risks.
- Competitive threats.
- Execution risks.
- Valuation risks.

## 9. Scenario comparison

Build a separate bear, base and bull case for each company.

Compare:
- Potential business performance.
- Valuation sensitivity.
- Downside exposure.
- Upside drivers.
- Key assumptions.

## 10. Investment attractiveness

Create a transparent comparison framework using:

- Business quality.
- Financial strength.
- Growth potential.
- Cash-flow quality.
- Competitive advantage.
- Valuation.
- Balance sheet risk.
- Management execution.

Explain the evidence behind every assessment.

Do not give an arbitrary score without explaining the methodology.

## 11. Final deliverables

Produce:

1. Sector overview.
2. Business comparison.
3. Financial comparison dashboard.
4. Profitability comparison.
5. Cash-flow comparison.
6. Growth comparison.
7. Relative valuation table.
8. DCF comparison.
9. Risk comparison.
10. Bull/base/bear scenario comparison.
11. Key investment opportunities.
12. Key investment concerns.
13. Information gaps and unresolved conflicts.

The objective is to identify which companies deserve deeper consideration, not to automatically select the fastest-growing company.
