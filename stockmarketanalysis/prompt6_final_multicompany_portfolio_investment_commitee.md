# Prompt 6: Final Multi-Company Portfolio Investment Committee

Act as the Chief Investment Officer of an institutional investment fund.

I have researched four companies in the same sector.

You have access to:

- Four individual company research reports.
- Four five-year financial analyses.
- Four Excel valuation models.
- Four DCF valuations.
- Four competitor valuation reports.
- Sector comparative analysis.

My investment profile:

Investment capital: [AMOUNT]
Investment horizon: [1–3 YEARS]
Investor location: [COUNTRY]
Risk tolerance: [LOW / MODERATE / HIGH]
Investment objective: [OBJECTIVE]

Your task is to conduct the final investment committee assessment.

## 1. Challenge the existing research

- Verify financial consistency.
- Identify unsupported assumptions.
- Check historical financial figures.
- Review valuation methodologies.
- Identify conflicting conclusions.
- Review data freshness.

## 2. Compare investment opportunities

For each company, evaluate:

- Business quality.
- Growth sustainability.
- Financial strength.
- Competitive advantage.
- Cash-flow generation.
- Capital efficiency.
- Debt exposure.
- Management execution.
- Valuation attractiveness.

## 3. Risk-adjusted investment analysis

Do not focus only on expected returns.

Analyze:
- Downside scenarios.
- Volatility.
- Financial leverage.
- Valuation compression.
- Industry concentration.
- Correlation between companies.
- Currency exposure.

## 4. Portfolio allocation scenarios

Build three hypothetical allocation strategies:

A. Conservative:
Emphasize balance-sheet strength and downside resilience.

B. Balanced:
Combine growth, valuation and financial quality.

C. Growth-oriented:
Emphasize long-term growth opportunities while explicitly accounting for downside risks.

Include:
- Allocation percentage.
- Investment amount.
- Number of shares.
- Expected return assumptions.
- Downside scenarios.
- Concentration risk.

Do not assume that all capital must be invested.

## 5. Investment timing

Analyze:
- Current valuation.
- Upcoming earnings.
- Important catalysts.
- Financial milestones.
- Potential entry valuation ranges.
- Conditions that would justify waiting.

## 6. Final investment committee report

Produce:

1. Executive summary.
2. Comparative company analysis.
3. Valuation conclusions.
4. Risk-adjusted analysis.
5. Portfolio allocation scenarios.
6. Capital deployment plan.
7. Investment monitoring dashboard.
8. Thesis invalidation conditions.
9. Final unresolved questions.

## Important restrictions

- Do not automatically choose the fastest-growing company.
- Do not automatically choose the cheapest company.
- Do not rely on analyst price targets alone.
- Do not average incompatible valuation models.
- Do not guarantee returns.
- Include all relevant assumptions and data dates.
- Allow a conclusion of holding cash or waiting if the valuations and risks do not justify investing.

The final report must explain why each portfolio allocation is appropriate under its stated assumptions.
