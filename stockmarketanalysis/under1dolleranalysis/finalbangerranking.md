# FINAL PENNY STOCK BANGER FINDER & SWING TRADING STRATEGY

Act as a professional hedge fund trader, quantitative analyst, event-driven investor and risk manager.

I have completed the previous nine research stages.

Your task is to consolidate the research and select the strongest under-$1 US-listed penny-stock swing-trading candidates.

Do not introduce new stocks.

Use only the companies that passed the previous screening stages.

## 1. Final candidate comparison

Compare every surviving company using:

- Current share price.
- Market capitalization.
- Public float.
- Average trading volume.
- Recent price decline.
- Original reason for decline.
- Latest corporate developments.
- Turnaround progress.
- Financial survival.
- Cash runway.
- Debt.
- Dilution risk.
- Upcoming catalysts.
- Unusual trading volume.
- Technical setup.
- Support and resistance.
- Short interest, where available.
- Overall risk.

## 3. Final ranking

Classify:

TIER 1: Highest-priority swing candidate.
TIER 2: Strong watchlist.
TIER 3: Speculative momentum opportunity.
AVOID: Unacceptable fundamental, dilution or execution risk.

Select the five strongest candidates, even if fewer than five qualify.

## 5. Profit and loss scenarios

For every selected stock calculate:

- 10% price increase.
- 20% price increase.

Show the impact of trading costs and estimated slippage where data is available.

## 6. Portfolio risk management

Assume a configurable trading capital of $1,000.

Calculate:
- Position size.
- Maximum dollar risk.
- Shares to purchase.
- Stop-loss amount.
- Potential profit at each target.

Use a maximum risk of 0.5–1% of trading capital per trade as the initial risk-management assumption.

Never risk more than the predefined amount because a stock appears attractive.

Account for spreads, slippage, liquidity, trading halts and overnight gaps.

Do not assume stop-loss orders guarantee execution at the specified price.

## 8. Final decision

For each of the five strongest candidates answer:

1. Why did it crash?
2. What is management doing to improve the business?
3. Is the turnaround supported by actual evidence?
4. Is the company financially capable of surviving?
5. Is dilution a major threat?
6. What event could trigger a price surge?
7. Is buying interest increasing?
8. What technical confirmation is required?
9. What is the potential upside?
10. What would invalidate the trading thesis?

FINAL OBJECTIVE:

Identify a small number of high-quality research candidates among severely declining under-$1 stocks.

Prioritize verified corporate developments, improving financial conditions, credible catalysts, tradable liquidity and favorable risk/reward.

Do not force a trade.

If no stock meets the required standards, explicitly report NO TRADE.

The purpose is to find asymmetric swing-trading opportunities, not to predict or guarantee sudden price surges.
