# PHASE 1: DOWNLOAD AND ANALYZE FINANCIAL FILINGS

Retrieve the company's financial statements directly from SEC EDGAR and its official investor relations website.

Download and examine:

1. Last five completed fiscal years of 10-K annual reports.
2. All available quarterly 10-Q reports covering the last five years.
3. Latest available 10-Q.
4. Relevant 8-K filings.
5. Earnings releases and investor presentations.
6. Earnings call transcripts.
7. DEF 14A proxy statements.
8. Form 3, Form 4 and Form 5 insider ownership filings.
9. Relevant Form 13D, 13G and 13F institutional ownership filings.

Use these primary sources:
- https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/
- Company's official investor relations website.

For each filing:
- Record filing date and reporting period.
- Extract relevant financial statements.
- Identify accounting policy changes.
- Identify restatements or material adjustments.
- Identify unusual one-time items.
- Identify changes in reporting segments.
- Check for material weaknesses in internal controls.

Do not invent missing historical information.

If the five-year financial history is unavailable, explicitly state the available period.

# PHASE 3: FREE CASH FLOW ANALYSIS

Calculate free cash flow for every available fiscal year.

Use:

Free Cash Flow = Operating Cash Flow - Capital Expenditure

Provide a table containing:

| Financial year | Operating cash flow | CapEx | Free cash flow | FCF margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

Investigate:

- Is free cash flow consistently positive?
- Is operating cash flow growing?
- Is capital expenditure increasing?

Determine whether the company can finance its future growth internally.

# PHASE 4: BALANCE SHEET AND FINANCIAL SURVIVAL

Analyze:

- Cash and cash equivalents.
- Short-term investments.
- Long-term investments.
- Total debt.
- Short-term debt.
- Long-term debt.
- Lease liabilities.
- Net debt or net cash.
- Current assets.
- Current liabilities.
- Working capital.
- Current ratio.
- Quick ratio.
- Interest coverage ratio.
- Debt-to-equity ratio.
- Goodwill and intangible assets.
- Debt maturity schedule.

Calculate:

Net Debt = Total Debt - Cash and Cash Equivalents - Eligible Liquid Investments

Clearly explain which investments are genuinely liquid and available to repay debt.

For loss-making companies, calculate cash runway.

Cash Runway = Available Cash / Average Monthly Cash Burn

Use a suitable operating cash burn measure and explain its limitations.

Determine:
- Can the company survive for the next 24–36 months?
- Will it need additional funding?
- Is refinancing necessary?
- Is equity dilution likely?
- Is bankruptcy or restructuring a realistic risk?

## Method C: Historical valuation

Explain whether the previous valuation was justified by higher growth, profitability or market conditions.

# PHASE 11: RISK-REWARD ANALYSIS

Calculate:

- Current stock price.
- Bear-case fair value.
- Base-case fair value.
- Bull-case fair value.
- Expected return over one year.
- Expected return over three years.
- Maximum potential downside.
- Upside-to-downside ratio.
- Margin of safety.

Use:

Expected Value = (Bear Value × Bear Probability) + (Base Value × Base Probability) + (Bull Value × Bull Probability)

Expected Return = (Expected Value / Current Share Price) - 1

Also calculate annualized expected return over the three-year horizon.

Explain whether the potential reward adequately compensates for the risk.

# RESEARCH RULES

1. Prioritize SEC filings and official company disclosures.
2. Cross-check important numbers against financial statements.
3. Use the latest available stock price and clearly date it.
4. Clearly distinguish reported numbers from analyst estimates.
5. Never fabricate financial information.
6. Show formulas and calculations.
7. Identify missing or unavailable information.
8. Adjust for stock splits and share dilution.
9. Do not confuse GAAP and non-GAAP earnings.
10. Do not treat stock-based compensation as economically free.
11. Do not assume historical stock prices represent intrinsic value.
12. Do not recommend purchasing merely because the share price has fallen.
13. Cite sources for every important financial claim.
14. Include SEC filing links and official investor relations links.
15. State all financial amounts in USD.
