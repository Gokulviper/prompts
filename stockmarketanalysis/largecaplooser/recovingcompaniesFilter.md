# US LARGE-CAP STOCK RECOVERY DETECTOR
## Identify Fundamentally Strong Companies Recovering After Severe Stock Declines

Act as a senior institutional equity research analyst, distressed-value investor, forensic financial analyst and turnaround investment specialist.

I have already completed a market-wide screening exercise and identified US large-cap companies whose share prices have declined severely (typically 40% or more).

Your task is NOT to search for new losers.

Your task is to analyze the previously identified losing companies and filter out businesses that are fundamentally deteriorating.

I want to identify companies that:

1. Have experienced a significant stock-price decline.
2. Have fundamentally healthy businesses or credible recovery potential.
3. Are showing early signs of operational or financial improvement.
4. Have sufficient cash and financial stability to survive.
5. Have attractive valuations relative to their recovery potential.
6. Could deliver meaningful returns over the next 1–3 years.

The objective is to identify potential recovery investments, not simply cheap-looking stocks.

## INPUT

Use the output of my previous US Large-Cap Stock Crash Detector.

Analyze every company in that shortlist.

Do not introduce unrelated companies unless explicitly identifying a missing candidate that meets the original screening criteria.

# FILTER 1: ELIMINATE VALUE TRAPS

Investigate each company and identify serious warning signs.

Eliminate or downgrade companies exhibiting:

- Continuous revenue contraction.
- Declining gross margins.
- Deteriorating operating margins.
- Persistent negative free cash flow.
- Unsustainable cash burn.
- Excessive debt.
- Imminent refinancing requirements.
- Weak liquidity.
- Repeated equity dilution.
- Major customer losses.
- Declining market share.
- Accounting irregularities.
- Regulatory problems.
- Management credibility issues.
- Permanent competitive disadvantage.
- Products becoming obsolete.
- Irreversible AI disruption.
- Bankruptcy or restructuring risk.

Classify each company:

A. Eliminate – Severe fundamental deterioration.

B. High Risk – Survival or recovery is uncertain.

C. Watchlist – Fundamentals are mixed, requiring further evidence.

D. Recovery Candidate – Business fundamentals are stable or improving.

Explain the exact reason for every classification.

Do not eliminate companies solely because they are temporarily loss-making if their business has a credible path to profitability.

# FILTER 2: IDENTIFY SIGNS OF BUSINESS RECOVERY

This is the most important filter.

Analyze the latest four quarters against the previous four quarters and the corresponding periods from the previous year.

Look for positive changes.

## Revenue recovery

- Is revenue growing again?
- Has revenue contraction stopped?
- Is year-over-year growth accelerating?
- Are new customers being acquired?
- Are existing customers spending more?
- Is customer retention improving?
- Are orders and bookings increasing?
- Is the backlog growing?

## Profitability recovery

- Is gross margin improving?
- Is operating margin improving?
- Are operating losses narrowing?
- Is EBITDA improving?
- Is net income returning to profitability?
- Are costs growing more slowly than revenue?

## Cash flow recovery

- Is operating cash flow improving?
- Is free cash flow turning positive?
- Is cash burn decreasing?
- Is working capital improving?
- Is capital expenditure becoming more efficient?

## Business recovery

- Are new products generating revenue?
- Are major customers returning?
- Are customer cancellations decreasing?
- Are market-share losses slowing?
- Is management successfully executing its turnaround plan?

Calculate the direction of each metric.

Classify the trend as:
- Strong improvement.
- Moderate improvement.
- Stable.
- Deteriorating.

Do not confuse management promises with demonstrated financial improvements.

# FILTER 6: STOCK PRICE AND TECHNICAL RECOVERY

Analyze stock-price behavior separately from business fundamentals.

Investigate:

- Current price relative to the 52-week low.
- Current price relative to the 52-week high.
- One-month performance.
- Three-month performance.
- Six-month performance.
- Relative performance versus the S&P 500 and sector benchmark.
- Trading volume.
- Short interest.
- Analyst estimate revisions.
- Earnings reaction.

Identify:

1. Stocks forming a potential bottom.
2. Stocks showing sustained recovery.
3. Stocks still making lower lows.
4. Stocks experiencing short-lived relief rallies.

Technical signals are supporting evidence, not proof of fundamental recovery.

# FILTER 7: VALUATION AND RECOVERY UPSIDE

For each surviving company, examine:

- Current P/E.
- Forward P/E.
- Price-to-sales.
- EV/EBITDA.
- Price-to-free-cash-flow.
- Historical valuation multiples.
- Competitor valuation multiples.
- Free cash flow yield.

Estimate:

- Bear-case fair value.
- Base-case fair value.
- Bull-case fair value.

Use reasonable assumptions for revenue growth, operating margins, cash generation and valuation multiples.

Calculate:

Potential Upside = (Estimated Fair Value / Current Price - 1) × 100

Determine whether the stock is genuinely undervalued or merely cheaper than before.
