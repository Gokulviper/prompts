# US LARGE-CAP STOCK CRASH DETECTOR & DEEP EQUITY RESEARCH ANALYST

Act as a senior Wall Street equity research analyst, hedge fund analyst, forensic financial analyst, and institutional investment researcher.

Your mission is to identify publicly traded US large-cap companies listed on NASDAQ that have experienced severe and sudden stock price declines of 40% or more.

I am looking for potentially undervalued, fundamentally strong companies that have suffered significant market sell-offs, unexpected negative events, temporary business challenges, or market overreactions.

I want to discover companies that could potentially recover over the next 1–3 years.

This is a research and stock-discovery exercise, NOT a recommendation to buy every declining stock.

## 1. STOCK SCREENING: FIND ALL MAJOR LOSERS

Search across ALL industry sectors, without limiting the search to technology companies.

Include:

- Technology
- Artificial Intelligence
- Semiconductors
- Cloud Computing
- Software / SaaS
- Cybersecurity
- Financial Services
- Banking / Fintech
- Healthcare
- Biotechnology
- Pharmaceuticals
- Consumer Discretionary
- Consumer Staples
- E-commerce
- Retail
- Industrials
- Manufacturing
- Energy
- Renewable Energy
- Telecommunications
- Media & Entertainment
- Automotive / EV
- Aerospace & Defense
- Real Estate
- Other publicly traded industries

### Stock eligibility criteria

1. Exchange: NASDAQ-listed US equities.
2. Market capitalization: Minimum $10 billion.
3. Include large-cap and mega-cap companies.
4. Exclude ETFs, leveraged ETFs, penny stocks, warrants, and SPACs.
5. Include companies regardless of whether they are profitable or currently loss-making.

### Identify stocks meeting these decline criteria:

**Category A – Major crashes**
- Down 40% or more from their 52-week high.

**Category B – Recent sudden crashes**
- Down 40% or more within the last 30 trading days.

**Category C – Severe short-term declines**
- Down 25% or more within the last 5–10 trading days.

**Category D – Extended crashes**
- Down 50%, 60%, 70%, or more from their all-time or 52-week highs.

Do not mix these categories. Clearly identify which criterion each company meets.

Find as many qualifying companies as possible. Do not restrict the results to a predetermined list of popular stocks.

Use the latest available market data and specify the exact data date.

## 3. DEEP ANALYSIS OF EACH COMPANY

For every qualifying company, conduct detailed research.

### A. Company fundamentals

- What does the company do?
- How does it generate revenue?
- What are its main products and services?
- Who are its major customers?
- What is its competitive advantage?
- Does it have a strong market position?
- Is the business model sustainable?
- What is its addressable market?
- Who are its main competitors?

### B. Why did the stock suddenly crash?

This is the most important section.

Investigate the actual reasons behind the stock decline.

Look for:

- Poor quarterly earnings.
- Revenue growth slowdown.
- Declining profit margins.
- Weak future guidance.
- Customer losses.
- Major contract cancellations.
- Debt problems.
- Cash flow deterioration.
- Accounting irregularities.
- Fraud allegations.
- Management departures.
- Regulatory investigations.
- Government restrictions.
- Lawsuits.
- Product failures.
- Industry disruption.
- Competitor threats.
- AI disruption.
- Insider selling.
- Institutional selling.
- Analyst downgrades.
- Valuation corrections.
- Macroeconomic conditions.

Identify the exact events and dates.

Distinguish between confirmed facts, management statements, analyst opinions, and market speculation.

Explain whether the decline is caused by:
1. Temporary problems.
2. Structural business deterioration.
3. Overvaluation correction.
4. Financial distress.
5. Industry-wide weakness.
6. Unexpected negative news.
7. Market panic or overreaction.

Do not assume a stock is undervalued just because it has fallen 40%.

### C. Financial statement analysis

Analyze the latest 5 years of financial statements and the most recent quarterly results.

**Income statement**
- Revenue growth.
- Gross profit margin.
- Operating margin.
- Net income.
- EPS growth.
- Research and development expenses.

**Balance sheet**
- Cash and cash equivalents.
- Total debt.
- Net debt.
- Current assets and liabilities.
- Goodwill and intangible assets.
- Debt maturity schedule.

**Cash flow**
- Operating cash flow.
- Free cash flow.
- Capital expenditure.
- Cash burn.
- Stock-based compensation.

Determine whether the company has enough financial strength to survive its current difficulties.

### D. Valuation analysis

Evaluate:

- P/E ratio.
- Forward P/E.
- Price-to-sales ratio.
- EV/EBITDA.
- Price-to-free-cash-flow.
- Price-to-book where relevant.
- Historical valuation multiples.
- Competitor valuation comparison.

Determine whether the current stock price is genuinely attractive or whether the stock remains expensive despite its decline.

Compare current valuation with its historical averages and industry peers.

### E. Management and insider activity

Investigate:

- CEO and CFO track record.
- Management credibility.
- Insider buying and selling.
- Institutional ownership changes.
- Major shareholder activity.
- Recent 13F filings.
- Share dilution.
- Stock-based compensation.

Identify whether insiders are buying shares during the decline or selling them.

Do not treat insider selling alone as proof of negative fundamentals.

### F. Competitive positioning

- Is the company losing market share?
- Are competitors taking customers?
- Is its technology becoming outdated?
- Is AI disrupting its business?
- Does it have pricing power?
- Can competitors replace its products?
- Does it have a sustainable competitive moat?

### G. Future growth potential

Investigate potential recovery drivers:

- New products.
- New markets.
- AI opportunities.
- Product launches.
- Customer acquisition.
- Revenue acceleration.
- Cost reductions.
- Debt reduction.
- Industry recovery.
- Regulatory changes.
- Strategic partnerships.

Identify realistic catalysts over the next 12–36 months.

## 4. IDENTIFY VALUE TRAPS

Be extremely critical.

Identify companies that have fallen significantly but may continue declining.

Red flags include:

- Continuous revenue contraction.
- Negative operating cash flow.
- Unsustainable debt.
- Liquidity problems.
- Repeated equity dilution.
- Accounting concerns.
- Losing competitive advantage.
- Permanent customer losses.
- Management credibility problems.
- Bankruptcy or restructuring risk.
- Irreversible industry disruption.

Clearly explain why a company might never recover to its previous stock price.

## 5. RECOVERY SCENARIO ANALYSIS

For each promising company, prepare three scenarios.

| Scenario | Description |
|---|---|
| Bear case | Business continues deteriorating |
| Base case | Business stabilizes and grows moderately |
| Bull case | Business recovers and achieves strong growth |

For each scenario, estimate:

- Revenue growth assumptions.
- Profitability assumptions.
- Expected valuation multiples.
- Estimated share price.
- Potential upside or downside.
- Main catalysts.
- Expected time horizon.

Use transparent assumptions and explain the valuation methodology.

Never present speculative price targets as guaranteed outcomes.

## 6. FINAL STOCK RANKING

After analyzing the qualifying companies, rank the 15 most interesting research candidates.

Use this scoring framework:

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Business quality | 20% |
| Financial strength | 20% |
| Valuation attractiveness | 20% |
| Recovery potential | 15% |
| Competitive advantage | 10% |
| Management quality | 5% |
| Risk profile | 10% |

Score each company out of 100.

Classify each stock:

- Tier 1: Strong business, attractive valuation, credible recovery potential.
- Tier 2: Interesting opportunity, but significant uncertainty.
- Tier 3: Speculative recovery opportunity.
- Avoid: Value trap or serious financial deterioration.

Deliver the research in the following order:

4. Top 15 stocks with the largest declines.
5. Deep financial analysis of the most promising 15 companies.
6. Detailed reasons behind each stock's crash.
7. Value traps and companies to avoid.
8. Bull, base, and bear recovery scenarios.
9. Final ranking based on investment attractiveness.
10. Five companies worth investigating further.

For each of the five final candidates, answer:

- Why has the stock fallen?
- Is the decline justified?
- Is the business fundamentally healthy?
- Is the company financially stable?
- Is the current valuation attractive?
- What could trigger a recovery?
- What could cause another major decline?
- What is the realistic 1–3 year investment opportunity?

## 8. RESEARCH QUALITY REQUIREMENTS

Use reliable, up-to-date sources:

- SEC EDGAR filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K).
- Official investor relations websites.
- Quarterly earnings reports.
- Earnings call transcripts.
- Nasdaq.
- Financial statements.
- Reputable financial news.
- Analyst reports where available.
- Institutional ownership filings.

Important rules:

1. Use current market prices and market capitalization.
2. Mention the exact date of every important financial metric.
3. Do not fabricate stock prices, financial ratios, earnings, or forecasts.
4. Provide source links for important claims.
5. Cross-check significant negative events.
6. Do not confuse a 40% decline from an all-time high with a 40% recent crash.
7. Include delisted or bankrupt companies only in a separate historical cautionary section, not in the current stock shortlist.
8. If information is unavailable, explicitly state that.
9. Do not stop after identifying the first 10 companies. Aim for comprehensive market coverage.
10. Do not recommend a stock solely because it is down significantly.

**FINAL OBJECTIVE**

I want to discover high-quality large-cap companies that the market has punished severely, where the underlying business may still have strong long-term potential.

Think like a disciplined value investor searching for opportunities during periods of extreme market pessimism.

Prioritize business fundamentals, financial survival, valuation, and realistic recovery potential over the size of the stock price decline.
